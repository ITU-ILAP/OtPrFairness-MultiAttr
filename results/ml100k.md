All experiments use **BiasedMF** or **GNN** backbone on ml100k (943 users, 1,682 items).
Sensitive attributes: `u_gender`, `u_age`, `u_occupation`, `u_activity`.

### Key Finding

On ml100k, the result is the **opposite of insurance**. GNN None already starts at 0.91 AUC for `u_activity` — worse than BiasedMF None (0.90). GNN PCFR cannot reduce this; leakage stays at ~0.91 regardless of which attribute it targets. BiasedMF PCFR remains effective, cutting leakage from 0.78–0.90 down to 0.52–0.69.

## Why GNN Helps on Insurance but Hurts on ml100k

| | Insurance | ml100k |
|---|---|---|
| Items | 12 | 1,682 |
| Graph structure | Uniform interactions → sparse, demographically mixed graph | Rich item space → users cluster by interest → interest clusters correlate with demographics |
| Effect of GNN propagation | Averages mixed-demographic neighbours → embeddings become *less* attribute-informative | Amplifies homophilic demographic signals → embeddings become *more* attribute-informative |
| PCFR filter outcome | Small residual signal → easy to suppress | Structural encoding too deep → filter cannot overcome it |

On ml100k's dense graph, users with similar demographics naturally interact with similar items, forming demographic clusters. GNN propagation amplifies these clusters rather than diluting them. BiasedMF, with no graph structure, is immune to this effect and remains the more robust backbone for process fairness on real-world dense graphs.
